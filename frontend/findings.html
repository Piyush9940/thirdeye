Evidence-Based Analysis

WHAT CAN WE LEARN FROM THE AVAILABLE DATA?

Scientific integrity means celebrating what the data reveals while openly documenting its limits. Here is what the verified pilot observations show—and what they do not show.

Scientific Integrity Standard: No Fabricated Claims
We do not invent unverified emission sources, illegal leak accusations, or long-term multi-decade trends. The verified dataset consists of 4 discrete temporal snapshot months (May 2019, Feb 2021, Aug 2021, Nov 2023) across a 2° spatial grid.
1. Satellite Coverage Is Unequal 98 of 255 Nodes

Spaceborne observations are not evenly distributed. Out of 255 grid nodes mapped across India, only 98 unique nodes contain valid soundings passing quality filtering (QA ≥ 0.50).

Key Example: The Southeast study region currently has 0 valid soundings in this pilot release due to optical cloud cover. It is categorized as unavailable, never zero.
2. Monsoon Cloud Masking August 2021 Data Gap

During the peak Southwest monsoon in August 2021, deep convective cloud decks prevented optical satellite retrieval across almost the entire country.

Contrast: May 2019 yielded 82 observed nodes and Feb 2021 yielded 67, but August 2021 recorded only 11 nodes.
3. Climate Context Matters NASA POWER

Collocating surface meteorology (temperature, precipitation, humidity, wind) provides valuable context for examining methane variations, but correlations do not prove cause and effect.

Correlation ≠ Causality: Methane column abundance is governed by large-scale atmospheric transport and vertical mixing.
4. Four Snapshots, Not Continuous Time Pilot Scope

The pilot dataset contains observations from four specific calendar months: May 2019, Feb 2021, Aug 2021, and Nov 2023.

Limitation: These discrete points are sufficient for spatial exploratory analysis, but cannot establish a continuous multi-year national trend line.

What the Data Supports What Cannot Be Claimed
Valid spatial variations in atmospheric column-averaged concentration (XCH₄ in ppb). Pinpointing exact ground-level emission sources or industrial leak points.
Significant seasonal reduction in optical satellite soundings during monsoon months. Assuming zero concentration in regions where observations were obscured by clouds.
Collocated meteorological context from NASA POWER (T2M, PRECTOTCORR, RH2M, WS10M). Proving direct causality between surface temperature and column concentration.
Benchmarking experimental machine learning architectures under rigorous chronological validation. Marketing experimental models (with negative R²) as operational forecasting tools.
