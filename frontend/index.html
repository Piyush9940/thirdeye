Atmospheric Dynamics

A SMALL GAS WITH A BIG CLIMATE IMPACT.

Methane (CH₄) is a greenhouse gas that traps heat in Earth's atmosphere. Over a 20-year timescale, it possesses more than 80 times the warming potential of carbon dioxide. Studying its atmospheric concentration helps researchers understand an important part of our changing climate.

Atmospheric Concentration

How much methane is present in a given volume of ambient atmosphere (measured in parts per billion, ppb). This is what satellites observe.

Methane Emissions

The rate at which methane is released into the air from sources. Our project studies atmospheric concentration, not direct emission rates.

Target Region

TARGET REGION: INDIA

Exploring available atmospheric methane observations and climate information across the Indian subcontinent [7°N – 37°N, 69°E – 97°E].

India's dynamic monsoon cycles, agricultural rhythms, diverse terrain, and tropical climate create distinct atmospheric patterns that make it an essential focus for Earth observation research.

Geographical Partition

SIX STUDY REGIONS & 255 NODES

To understand geographic variations, the subcontinent is mapped onto a 255-node spatial grid partitioned into six zones based on geographical boundaries:

1. Northwest Lat ≥ 25°N, Lon < 82°E
2. Northeast Lat ≥ 25°N, Lon ≥ 82°E
3. Central-West 15° ≤ Lat < 25°, Lon < 82°
4. Central-East 15° ≤ Lat < 25°, Lon ≥ 82°
5. Southwest Lat < 15°N, Lon < 82°E
6. Southeast ⚠️ Unavailable in Pilot Data
*Scientific Note: The Southeast region has 0 valid soundings in this pilot release due to cloud cover and QA filtering (< 0.50). It is displayed as an area with unavailable observations, not zero concentration.

Comprehensive Story

From Satellite Orbit to Public Understanding

Follow the complete research journey designed to make Earth observation and climate science accessible to everyone.

1. Our Planet Is Changing Earth Systems

Human activities, natural ecosystems, and shifting atmospheric circulation are driving worldwide environmental transformations. Continuous satellite monitoring provides the broad spatial perspective needed to observe these changes.

2. Why Methane Matters 80x Warming

Methane accounts for ~30% of global warming since the pre-industrial era. With an atmospheric lifetime of roughly 12 years, actions addressing methane can yield rapid climate benefits compared to longer-lived gases.

Read More About Methane
3. How Satellites Observe Earth Sentinel-5P

The Copernicus Sentinel-5P satellite carries the TROPOMI spectrometer in a 824-km polar orbit. It measures solar radiation reflected from Earth's surface and atmosphere across the shortwave infrared (SWIR) absorption band.

Satellite Explainer
4. Why We Are Studying India Regional Study

India represents a vital cross-section of tropical meteorology, intense seasonal monsoons, agricultural rice paddy wetlands, and diverse topographic corridors spanning from the Himalayas to the Indian Ocean.

Explore India Map
5. What Information Was Collected Verified Exports
