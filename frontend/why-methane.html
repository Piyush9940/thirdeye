Atmospheric Science Explainer

WHY SHOULD WE CARE ABOUT METHANE?

A small atmospheric molecule with a major climate impact. Understanding what methane is, how it warms the planet, and why spaceborne satellites observe it.

Important Scientific Distinction: Concentration vs. Emissions
Atmospheric Methane Concentration: How much methane is present in a given column of ambient air (measured in parts per billion, ppb). This is what Sentinel-5P measures.
Methane Emissions: The rate at which methane is released into the air from sources (e.g. kg per hour).
Our project investigates atmospheric concentration patterns across India. It does not measure total emissions or pinpoint exact illegal emission sources.
1. Potent Heat-Trapping Gas 84x Impact

Methane (CH₄) is an exceptionally potent greenhouse gas. Over a 20-year timescale, a ton of methane traps over 80 times more thermal heat in Earth's atmosphere than a ton of carbon dioxide.

2. Short Atmospheric Lifetime ~12 Years

Unlike carbon dioxide which lingers in the atmosphere for centuries, methane breaks down in approximately 12 years through chemical reactions with tropospheric hydroxyl radicals (•OH). Reducing methane delivers rapid near-term climate benefits.

3. One-Third of Modern Warming ~30%

Scientific assessments by the IPCC and NASA show that atmospheric methane is responsible for roughly 30% of global temperature rise since the industrial revolution, making it the second most important anthropogenic climate forcer.

4. Broad Planetary Monitoring Sentinel-5P

Because methane mixes across large geographical distances, ground sensors only capture localized points. Spaceborne satellites provide the wide-area perspective needed to observe regional atmospheric dynamics across continents.

How Greenhouse Gases Trap Heat

Solar radiation warms Earth's surface. In return, the surface radiates thermal infrared energy back toward space. Greenhouse gas molecules absorb specific infrared wavelengths, exciting their molecular bonds and re-radiating heat in all directions, including back toward the surface.

Space Technology

HOW DO WE OBSERVE EARTH FROM SPACE?

From 824 kilometres in orbit to calibrated environmental data: the five-stage scientific pipeline.

1. Satellite Orbit Sentinel-5P

The Copernicus Sentinel-5 Precursor satellite flies in a sun-synchronous polar orbit, crossing the equator at ~13:30 local solar time to maximize daylight solar backscatter.

2. TROPOMI Sensor SWIR Band

The TROPOspheric Monitoring Instrument measures absorption spectra in the shortwave infrared (2305–2385 nm) band, detecting absorption signatures of atmospheric methane molecules.

3. Quality Filtering QA ≥ 0.50

Soundings contaminated by dense clouds, aerosol scattering, snow, or high surface roughness are filtered out. Our pilot dataset retains 73,045 high-confidence sub-pixel soundings.

4. Spatial Grid 2° Resolution

Soundings are geolocated and aggregated onto 255 regular spherical grid nodes across India, yielding 226 verified node-month observations across 4 snapshot periods.

5. Climate Integration NASA POWER

Observations are joined with surface meteorology: 2-meter air temperature (T2M), precipitation (PRECTOTCORR), relative humidity (RH2M), and 10-meter wind speed (WS10M).

6. Public Exploration Interactive Web

Presented through intuitive 3D and 2D interfaces allowing researchers, students, and citizens to explore environmental patterns freely and transparently.
